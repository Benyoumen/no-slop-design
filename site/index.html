No-slop design

Visual AI slop is manageable.

AI builds a screen in minutes, and the default result looks like every other AI screen. It costs credibility the moment a customer, a sponsor or a candidate opens it. Three screens for a fictional sales assistant, each built with a small set of rules, next to the version most tools would produce from the same brief. Both are live pages: open them, click, press Tab.

Fictional company, people and numbers. Snapshot of Monday, 13 April 2026. Every page is generated from the same data file by one script; nothing is edited by hand.

The rep's daily list

A field rep between two meetings, two minutes, laptop or phone: what do I owe the CRM today?

What to notice (numbers are the tells in the list)

  • 1, 2, 6No hero, no icon grid, nothing centered. The first thing on the screen is today's meetings in time order, on real-looking data with its date.
  • 7Three amounts and one stage read "no data" because the CRM has none. The generic version rounds them to the nearest five thousand.
  • 11Empty, loading and error are one click away in the state switcher. The generic page has one state.
  • 15The button says what happens in the system: "Save to CRM", with Undo for ten seconds. Not "Approve" with a check mark.
  • agentThe agent's proposal sits in its own column with the quote it is based on. A proposal never shares a cell with a recorded fact.

One proposal, reviewed

The same rep decides whether to let the agent write a stage change and a next step into the CRM.

What to notice (numbers are the tells in the list)

  • 4, 5No "heating up", no confidence score with no definition, no fire emoji. Recorded value next to proposed value, field by field.
  • agentEvidence: the two sentences from the notes the proposal is based on, with their timestamps. The generic version asks for trust without showing why.
  • 10Two buttons, one link. Approve writes and shows Undo. Edit makes the proposed values editable in place. Reject is not a red button; "Not now" lives on the list.
  • agentThe trail at the bottom: what the agent did before, when, approved by whom, and one entry that was undone.
  • 15A short "the agent will not" line where the decision is made, not in a footer.

The manager's week, on a phone

A sales manager on Friday afternoon, phone in hand: did my team update the CRM after their meetings, and who needs a call?

What to notice (numbers are the tells in the list)

  • 1, 14No health score, no medals, no confetti. One number with its label above and its reading below: last week, the change, the target.
  • 7One rep reads "no data" with the reason in one line. The generic version gives him a bronze medal and 40%.
  • 12The "needs attention" list is content-sized: days without activity, the recorded stage and amount, one link per deal.
  • 6, 9Left-aligned, one spacing scale, phone width first. Nothing is centered because nothing is a poster.
  • 13Every control is reachable with a keyboard and the focus is visible. Try Tab on both versions.

How these were made

  1. Real data, frozen. Twelve meetings and deals, anonymized, dated 13 April 2026. Three amounts and one stage are missing in the source, so the screens say so.
  2. One generator, never a hand edit. A single Python script reads the data and the tokens and writes every page here, including the generic twins.
  3. Measured, not squinted. Contrast on every text pair, two widths, keyboard reach, the four states. The tells list is the audit.

The rules: sixteen visual tells and what to do instead. The workflow: from a brief to a validated decision in a thirty-minute session. The skills: html-mockup, visual-slop-audit, visual-validation-session, design-tokens-starter. The tokens behind every screen: tokens.css.
